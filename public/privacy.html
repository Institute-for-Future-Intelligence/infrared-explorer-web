Institute for Future Intelligence, Inc. Effective September 1, 2026 Last updated November 17, 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how Institute for Future Intelligence, Inc. (“IFI”, “we”, “us”) collects, uses, and shares information when you use Infrared Explorer: the mobile app for Android and iOS (shown on your device as “IR Explorer”) and the companion website at ie.intofuture.org (together, the “Service”). IFI is a non-profit educational research organization based in Natick, Massachusetts, USA.

Infrared Explorer is a scientific instrument for thermal imaging with a FLIR ONE camera and a citizen-science tool for building the crowd-sourced Infrared Street View. It is not a medical device and is not intended for measuring body temperature or for any medical, health, or veterinary purpose.

The short version

  • You can use the app without an account. Everything you capture stays on your device unless you choose to share, export, or upload it, or to hand it to the AI in an experiment.
  • Signing in (Google, or Apple on iOS) gives us your name, email address, and profile picture, and lets you upload recordings and street views to the cloud. The app can also give you an anonymous account, which tells us none of these and is only for working with the AI in experiments.
  • Uploads are meant to be seen. Recordings are “link only” by default; street views are public on the shared map and include the precise location where you captured them. An experiment is private until you share it; sharing it shows the whole conversation with the AI.
  • Location is used only while the app is open, never in the background, and is sent to us only inside a street view you upload.
  • The AI works only when you ask it to. In an experiment, and in the AI tools on a recording’s page on the website, what you hand to the AI — thermal data, thermal images, visible-light photographs, photos you add, and what you type — is sent to the AI provider whose model you pick. You are told what will be sent and asked to agree before the first time. Your email address and account identifier are never sent; section 1.5 says where a name can be.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics SDKs, no sale of personal information.
  • You can delete your account in the app, on the website at ie.intofuture.org/delete-account, or by asking us. Deletion removes your account and everything it uploaded; section 6 lists the few things that can remain.

1. Information we collect

1.1 Account information (optional)

The app works fully as a guest, offline. If you sign in so you can upload to the cloud, join class activities, or use the website, what we receive depends on how you sign in:

  • Google Sign-In (Android, iOS, web): your name, email address, profile picture URL, and a Google account identifier used to authenticate you.
  • Sign in with Apple (iOS): your name (Apple provides it once, at first sign-in) and either your email address or, if you choose “Hide My Email”, an Apple private-relay address that forwards to you.
  • An anonymous account (app only): Continue Anonymously creates an account without asking who you are. We receive no name, email address, or profile picture — only the account identifier created for it. An anonymous account can create experiments and work with the AI in them (section 1.5), and can join a class (section 1.4); it cannot upload, share, rate, or use the website. It lives in the app on that phone and nowhere else: we have no way to sign you in to it again, so if you remove it from the app or lose the app’s data without deleting the account first, its experiments stay on our servers and neither you nor we can tell which were yours (section 6). You can link it to a Google or Apple account at any time; it then becomes an ordinary account and keeps its experiments.

We create an internal account identifier for you and keep an account record containing your email address, display name, profile picture URL, sign-in provider identifier, account creation time, and (on the website) the time of your most recent sign-in and your notification preferences. A separate public profile — display name, profile picture, the short bio you may write, and the month you joined — is visible to anyone on the website, signed in or not, together with the content you have made public. An anonymous account has neither: our sign-in provider keeps only its identifier and the times it was created and last used.

On your device the app keeps a list of the accounts you have signed in with (name, email, picture URL, identifiers) so you can switch between them, and keeps each account’s captures and settings separate.

1.2 Content you upload

Nothing is uploaded until you choose to. When you choose Upload in the app, share an experiment (section 1.5), or save, copy, or annotate a recording on the website, we store:

  • Thermal recordings: the radiometric thermal frames, the rendered thermal images, and the visible-light photographs taken at the same instants. The app always records the visible-light photographs alongside the thermal frames, and there is no setting to leave them out of an upload. Visible-light photographs may show people, homes, vehicles, and other identifiable details of the scene you filmed.
  • Photo sets: still photos you tick in the gallery and upload together as one item. A photo that carries temperature data uploads that data together with its rendered images; a photo without it — a screenshot-style capture, a chart, or a picture you brought in from your phone’s photo library — uploads as the picture alone. A picture brought in from your photo library is uploaded as it stands: we do not strip metadata your phone wrote into it (see Location).
  • Street views: the same kinds of images, plus the precise location (latitude and longitude) where the panorama was captured, the compass heading and tilt of each shot, and the capture time.
  • Descriptions: the title, description, subject, temperature unit, palette, and other settings you choose, and your display name shown as the author.
  • What you add to a recording: temperature probes, transects, and annotations and notes — those you placed in the app before uploading and those you add on the website — and, on the website, key-moment captions, chart settings, and the reports you generate for a recording.

A still photo is uploaded only when you select it and choose Upload yourself. Photos you do not upload stay on your device, and leave it only if you share them or turn on the System gallery setting, which copies captures into your phone’s photo library.

Who can see uploads. A recording you upload is link only (“unlisted”) by default: it does not appear in public lists, but anyone who has its link can open it, and it can appear as a “related” item next to other copies and clips of the same recording. You can change it to private or public on the website. A street view you upload is public: it appears on the shared Infrared Street View map for everyone, with its location, your display name, and its capture time. You control it afterwards: in the app, Account › Street View › My street views lets you make any of your street views private or delete it outright. If another user reports one of yours, it may be taken off the map automatically while we look at it, and its image files are moved at the same time to a location that is not publicly readable; we tell you when that happens, and you can appeal. Image and data files that are still published are served from web addresses that do not require sign-in; anyone who has such an address can open the file until it is deleted or moved.

Who can ask the AI about a recording. On the website, any signed-in user who can open a recording can ask the AI about it (section 1.5). For a recording you have made public or link only, that means its data can be sent to an AI provider at another user’s request, not only at yours. A private recording can be put to the AI only by you and by IFI staff.

1.3 Website activity

  • Comments you post on recordings, shown publicly with your display name and profile picture.
  • Ratings you give to recordings. Ratings are recorded under your account identifier and are publicly readable, so it is possible to tell which accounts rated a recording.
  • Viewing history: a private record of the recordings you open, visible only to you.
  • Notifications about comments and ratings on your recordings, which name the person who left them. You can turn these off in Settings.
  • Contact and deletion-request forms: the email address and message you type (and, on the contact form, your name), which are also emailed to us.

1.4 Classroom features (website and app)

If you join a class with a class number and password, your display name and email address are added to the class roster, which is visible to the teacher and to the other members of that class. The teacher’s name and email address are visible to class members. Your submissions to assignments are visible to the teacher; grades are written by the teacher and visible to you; work the teacher promotes to the class showcase is visible to the whole class; your private workspace is visible only to you.

In the app, any account — an anonymous one included — can join a class from the Account screen and hand in an experiment’s report to it. An anonymous account has no name or email address to put on the roster, so the roster shows the name you type when you first hand a report in. Handing in stores a copy of the report with the class: its title, text, and pictures, the name you typed, which AI model the experiment ran on, and when you handed it in. The teacher can read that copy; the experiment itself and its conversation are not shared with the teacher. The copy stays as you handed it in if you later change or delete the experiment; handing the same experiment in again replaces it, and you can take it back.

1.5 Experiments and other AI features

An experiment is a conversation with an AI model about something you want to find out with the camera. The AI helps you plan it, looks at the recordings and photos you add to it (its “captures”), and writes a report with you. You can work on experiments in the app and on the website. They need an account — any account, including an anonymous one in the app (section 1.1) — and are not offered to guests. The website also has two AI tools on a recording’s own page, Ask AI and AI Report, which any signed-in account can use.

Nothing goes to an AI provider until you act: you send a message, hand a capture to the AI, or ask for a report. Before the first time, the app or the website tells you what will be sent and to which provider, and asks you to agree.

What an experiment sends.

  • What you type: your messages, the experiment’s title, and the changes you make by hand to its plan and its report.
  • A summary of each capture you hand over, worked out on your phone or in your browser: the camera model, the emissivity setting, when it was captured, its title and description, the names and readings of its thermometers and transects, a condensed history of its temperatures, the notes pinned to the picture, and the moments the camera moved or recalibrated. From the app, the summary also carries three details saved in the capture: the name saved as its owner, which the app fills in for you when you capture — your display name, or the part of your email address before the @ if you have none, or “Anonymous” for an anonymous account; the thermographer’s name, if you typed one into the capture’s details; and the camera’s name with its serial number. A summary worked out on the website carries none of the three.
  • Pictures: a sheet of small thermal images covering the capture; the thermal image and the visible-light photograph at each moment the AI asks to look at; the photos you add to the experiment from your phone’s photo library or take for it with your phone’s camera; and the charts the AI draws. Visible-light photographs and your own photos may show people and other identifiable details.
  • Results of calculations: the AI can write short programs that run on your phone or in your browser over a capture’s temperature data. Only what they work out — numbers and charts — is sent back.

Not sent: your email address, your account identifier, and a capture’s location file. The frames of a capture that the AI did not ask to see stay on your phone, unless you upload the capture or share the experiment.

Who receives it. We pass the conversation — text and pictures — to the provider of the model you chose for that experiment: OpenAI, Google, xAI, or DeepSeek. Apart from what the conversation itself contains, the request carries no identifier for you or your account. When the model is OpenAI’s, it may search the web while it works; OpenAI runs those searches, with queries the model writes from the conversation.

What we store. The experiment is stored under your account: its title, plan, and report, the whole conversation (including the capture summaries above) and the pictures in it, and a list of its captures. From the app, that list includes the name and folder of each capture’s file on your phone and the installation identifier described in section 1.6. We keep an experiment until you delete it or delete your account.

Who can see an experiment. Only you, until you share it. Link Only lets anyone who has the link open it, with or without an account; Public also lists it in Community in the app and on the website. Whoever opens it gets a read-only copy — the title, plan, and report, the whole conversation and its pictures, and your display name — and, if signed in, can save a copy of it to their own account. The conversation is handed over as it is stored, so the capture summaries in it, including the owner name and camera serial number the app writes into them, are part of what a viewer’s app or browser receives even though the page does not display them. Turning sharing on in the app also uploads the experiment’s captures as link-only recordings (section 1.2), and while sharing is on, a capture you add is uploaded as you add it. Those recordings stay in your account when you turn sharing off. An anonymous account cannot share.

Ask AI and AI Report on the website. On a recording’s page, your browser works out the same kind of summary and pictures from the recording’s uploaded copy — without an owner name or a camera serial number — and sends them, with your question, any moments of the recording you attach as pictures of the viewer, and any notes you type for a report, to the provider of the model you pick (OpenAI, Google, xAI, or DeepSeek). You can ask about any recording you can open: your own, or someone else’s that is public or link only. Your Ask AI conversation about a recording is stored with that recording, where only you can read it, and you can clear it. Only a recording’s owner can have an AI Report written. The report, the pictures in it, and the notes typed to produce it are stored with the recording and visible to anyone who can view that recording; the working conversation behind a report is deleted automatically after seven days.

Limits and your agreement. Personal accounts — every account other than an IFI staff account — can make a limited number of AI requests each hour and each day, and anonymous accounts fewer. The counts behind these limits are kept under your account identifier and expire about a day after your last request. When you agree to send your data to an AI provider, we keep a record of that agreement (which version of the notice you agreed to, when, and the platform and app version you used) until you delete your account.

AI tools limited to IFI staff. Two further AI tools on the website — a lab assistant and a builder of 3D “digital twin” scenes — can at present be used only by IFI staff accounts. When a staff member uses them on a recording they are permitted to view, that recording’s thermal data, rendered thermal images, visible-light photographs, title, description, and annotations may be sent to a third-party AI provider (OpenAI, Google, xAI, DeepSeek, or Anthropic). What a twin builder produces is stored with the recording and visible to anyone who can view it.

AI lab reports from earlier versions of the app. Versions of the app from before October 2026 could write an “AI lab report” for a single uploaded recording, as earlier versions of this Policy described. Experiments have replaced that feature and it is no longer offered. A report written that way is still stored with its recording and visible to anyone who can view that recording, until its owner clears it or deletes the recording.

1.6 Diagnostic and technical information

  • Website error reports. If a page of the website crashes, the browser sends us the error message and stack trace, the page address, your browser type, and your account identifier if you are signed in. We keep these reports for 90 days; deletion runs as an automatic sweep, so a report can remain for up to a day after that.
  • Abuse prevention. To rate-limit view counting we store, for two hours, a one-way hash of your account identifier if you are signed in or of your IP address if you are not; to rate-limit the contact form we store a one-way hash of your IP address with a message count, for two hours; to rate-limit class joins and AI requests we store counts under your account identifier (the AI counts expire about a day after your last request). We do not store raw IP addresses in our database. These hashes are rate-limit keys, not anonymised data: an IP address is drawn from a small enough set of possibilities that we do not claim a hash of one cannot be reversed — what we do claim is that it is short-lived and is never attached to what you sent. Two details apply to every period named here and in section 5: the clock on a record restarts each time a new request is counted against it, and the deletion itself runs as an automatic sweep, so a record can persist up to a day beyond the period stated.
  • View counts on recordings are aggregate numbers; your own views of your own recordings are not counted.
  • The mobile app sends no analytics, crash reports, or advertising identifiers, and reads no identifier of your phone. Neither the app nor the website uses advertising or analytics SDKs, and we do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • Installation identifier. The app does make one identifier of its own: a random number, created the first time you use experiments while signed in, kept in the app’s storage, and replaced if you reinstall the app. It is sent to us with your experiments and stored in them, for one purpose: an experiment whose captures have not been uploaded is listed only on the phone that holds them. It is not sent to AI providers and is not used for anything else.
  • Server logs. Our hosting provider (Google Firebase) keeps standard infrastructure logs, including IP addresses and request timestamps, for a limited period to operate and secure the Service.

1.7 Information stored on your device or in your browser

The app stores your captures, the photos you add to experiments, settings, account list, pending uploads, the installation identifier described in section 1.6, and — if you use a wireless FLIR camera — the camera’s Wi-Fi password, in its own storage on your device. On iOS the capture folder is also visible in the Files app. The website keeps your sign-in session, display preferences, and unsent drafts in your browser’s local storage; it does not set advertising or tracking cookies.

1.8 Reports, flags, and blocks

The Infrared Street View map has no review before publication, so it is moderated by the people looking at it; the recordings and experiments people share, and what is posted in a class, can be reported the same way. These features record information as a result.

  • When you report a street view, a recording, an experiment, an author, or something in a class, we store what you reported (the street view or recording identifier, its title and its author’s account identifier at the time, or the reported author’s account identifier), the reason you picked, anything you typed in the description box, when you sent it, and how it was resolved. A report on a person in a class is filed against that person’s account, a report on a class as a whole against its teacher’s, and a report on an experiment someone shared against its author’s. The app adds one line to the description when the report needs more context: where a report on a recording is filed against the recording’s author, that line gives the recording’s title and web address; for a shared experiment it gives the experiment’s title and identifier; and where a report is made from inside a class, it gives the class number, the class name, and the class’s web address. If you were signed in, your account identifier is stored with it, so that we can tell you what happened and so that reporting the same thing twice does not count twice. For a report filed against a person’s account, “the same thing” means the same person. While a report you filed is still open, reporting the same thing again does not create a second report and cannot hide anything a second time — but if you have something new to say, what you type is kept on that open report as an addition, with the reason you picked and the time you sent it. A report holds up to ten additions; after that, or if you only repeat what you said last time, nothing further is stored and we ask you to write to us instead. Additions are deleted together with the report they were added to (section 5). Once a report has been resolved, reporting the same thing again starts a new report in place of the old one, additions and all. If you were not signed in, no identifier for you is stored on the report — neither your IP address nor anything derived from it. With no identifier there is nothing to match a later report against, so a report sent while signed out always stands on its own and never becomes an addition to an earlier one. A short-lived hash of your address is kept separately from the report, and only to limit how many reports can be sent in an hour; it expires about a day after your last report. Section 1.6 says what that hash is and is not.
  • Flags on AI lab reports. Earlier versions of the app let you flag an AI lab report from the report’s ⋮ menu. For each flag filed that way we store the recording’s identifier and title, its owner’s account identifier, a copy of the flagged report’s text as it stood when it was flagged, which model wrote it and when, the reason picked, anything typed in the description box, the account identifier of the person who flagged it, whether the recording is theirs, when it was sent, and how it was resolved. A flag does not hide the report, and the recording’s owner is not told about it. We keep the copy of the report so that we can judge what was flagged even if the report is later regenerated or cleared. If a flag is upheld, IFI staff may remove the report from the recording and may stop the owner’s account from uploading and from using the website’s AI tools. The current app and the website have no flag button: to tell us about something an AI wrote, report the experiment or recording it appears in, or write to us (section 12).
  • When you hide an author, we store that author’s account identifier and display name in a private list under your account, which only you and IFI staff can read. The author is never told. Removing them from the list deletes the entry.
  • What the author is told. If your street view or your recording is hidden or removed after a report, you get a notification and an email saying which one and what happened. Neither ever names who reported it. A public experiment that IFI staff remove from Community stays in your account and can no longer be made public.

2. Location

The app asks for permission to use your precise location, and only while the app is open. It never collects location in the background. Location is used to:

  • geotag a street view panorama so it appears in the right place on the shared map;
  • save a small location file next to each recording on your device, so a recording can later be placed on a map; this file stays on your device and is not uploaded or included in exports;
  • show your coordinates in the optional sensor overlay on the live view; and
  • center the street view map on where you are.

Your location reaches us only through something you send, and there are two such ways. The first is a street view you choose to upload, where the capture location becomes part of a public map pin; if a street view has no saved capture location, the app asks before using your current position instead. The second is a picture you brought in from your phone’s photo library, or took with your phone’s camera for an experiment, and then uploaded or handed to the AI: the app never writes location into a photo it takes, but it does not remove metadata a picture already carries, so if your phone geotagged that picture and hands the tag over when you pick it, the tag is part of what you send. Two things to know as well: a photo the app takes is a picture of what the screen shows, so if you have turned on the optional Sensor data overlay, the coordinates it displays are part of any photo you take while it is on, and travel with that photo if you share or upload it; and files you upload are served from web addresses that do not require sign-in. You can decline or later revoke the location permission in your device settings; the app keeps working, but street views cannot be placed on the map.

Camera hardware and connections

To operate a FLIR ONE camera the app uses USB, and for wireless FLIR ONE Edge cameras it uses Bluetooth, local-network discovery, and joins the camera’s own Wi-Fi network (only after you turn on the Wireless camera setting). Thermal images, and the visible-light detail blended into them, come from the FLIR camera’s own sensors, not from your phone’s camera. The app uses your phone’s camera in one place: in an experiment, Shoot from System Camera opens it so that you can add an ordinary photo to the conversation, where it is treated like any other picture you give the AI (section 1.5). The flashlight toggle also drives the phone camera’s LED. These capabilities are used solely to talk to the FLIR camera and to capture images; nothing about your Bluetooth or Wi-Fi surroundings is collected by us, and we do not send your data to Teledyne FLIR. The FLIR camera’s serial number is recorded in a capture’s local information file. It is not part of an upload; it leaves your phone only inside the capture summary the app sends when you hand that capture to the AI in an experiment (section 1.5).

3. How we use information

  • to operate, secure, and improve the Service and provide the features you use;
  • to authenticate you and associate your uploads, comments, and class memberships with your account;
  • to display your public content, profile, and street views to other users, as described above;
  • to run the AI features you ask for, by passing what you hand to the AI to the provider you chose;
  • to run the classroom features for teachers and students who use them;
  • to respond to your messages and deletion requests;
  • to enforce rate limits and prevent abuse;
  • to review reports about street views, recordings, experiments, authors, or anything in a class and flags on AI lab reports, to moderate content, and to act on violations of the Terms — including hiding or removing content and suspending accounts;
  • to conduct education research and report on the National Science Foundation grants that fund this work, using data in aggregate or de-identified form; and
  • to comply with law and protect the rights, safety, and property of users and IFI.

We do not sell personal information, use it for advertising, or use your content to train AI models of our own.

5. How long we keep information

  • Account and uploaded content: for as long as your account exists, or until you delete the content or the account.
  • Experiments, with their conversations and pictures: until you delete the experiment or your account. This holds for an anonymous account too — nothing expires on its own — so delete an anonymous account in the app when you are done with it (section 6).
  • Ask AI conversations on a recording: until you clear the conversation, the recording is deleted, or you delete your account. The working conversation behind an AI Report: seven days, plus up to a day for the sweep that deletes it. The report itself is kept with the recording.
  • Reports handed in to a class: until you take the report back, or the teacher deletes the class.
  • Recordings you move to the trash remain stored (and, if they were public or link-only, remain reachable by link) until you delete them permanently or delete your account.
  • Website error reports: 90 days, plus up to a day for the sweep that deletes them.
  • Abuse-prevention records: hashed view-counting records (account identifier or IP address) and hashed contact-form records expire two hours after the last request counted against them; AI request counters expire about a day after your last AI request; other per-account rate-limit counters are deleted with your account. As section 1.6 explains, the sweep that carries out these deletions can lag the stated period by up to a day.
  • Reports about street views, recordings, authors, or anything in a class, and flags on AI lab reports: 12 months from when they were filed, resolved or not, then deleted automatically. Anything added to a report that was still open (section 1.8) is deleted with the report it was added to, so an addition can be kept for less than 12 months and never for more. The rate-limit records behind them (a hash of an account identifier or IP address) expire about a day after the last report counted against them.
  • Lists of authors you have hidden: until you remove the entry or delete your account.

Deletion is immediate and permanent. It removes:

  • your account record, sign-in, profile, and public profile page;
  • every recording, photo set, and street view you uploaded, including thermal frames, images, and locations;
  • every experiment you created, with its plan, report, conversation, and pictures; your Ask AI conversations; and the AI reports on your recordings;
  • comments and ratings you left on other people’s recordings, together with any replies other users made to those comments;
  • the reports you filed about street views, recordings, authors, or class content, and your list of hidden authors; entries pointing at you in other users’ hidden-author lists are deleted too, so your account identifier does not survive as a bare string in a stranger’s list;
  • the flags you filed on AI lab reports, the flags anyone filed on AI reports about your recordings, and the records that you agreed to send data to AI providers;
  • your class memberships, submissions, grades, and workspace items; and
  • classes you created, together with everything inside them (your students’ own recordings are not affected).

Six things can remain, and we tell you so here rather than quietly:

  • if another user copied one of your recordings into their own account, the recording’s image files stay online so their work is not blanked out; your name, title, and account are still removed;
  • if another user saved a copy of an experiment you shared, their copy — the conversation and its pictures included — stays in their account;
  • reports you handed in to a class through the app stay with the class, under the name you typed, until the teacher deletes the class; take a report back before deleting your account if you do not want that;

Anything already downloaded, shared, or exported by you or by others before deletion is outside our control. Deleting your account does not delete captures stored on your own device; they are kept there and move to the guest library.
